What It Does

OCTAVE plays the music files you already own. You point it at a folder, and every sub-folder of that folder becomes a playlist. It reads each song's artist, album and cover from the file, remembers where you stopped, and carries on from there the next time the car starts.

It can also be the remote for Spotify (experimental): your Spotify playlists appear in the same list as your own, and the Spotify app on your phone does the playing. A mirrored phone or Android Auto can take over the music too; those are described on the Phone Mirror & Android Auto page.

This page starts with how to use it and gets more technical as it goes. Stop reading whenever you have what you came for.

Adding Your Music

With no music, the music screens and Home's music card show No Music Loaded and a Connect Music button.

  1. Put your songs in one folder on the device. Sort them into sub-folders if you want playlists: a folder named Road Trip becomes the playlist "Road Trip".
  2. Tap Connect Music, then Local Music, and pick that folder. (The same sheet offers Spotify and Phone where they are available.)
  3. OCTAVE reads the folder at once. The songs appear in the music library.

You can change the folder later in Settings > Media > Library & Playback > Library Folder: type a path or tap Browse. Until you pick one, OCTAVE uses your system's Music folder. After copying new songs into the folder, tap Scan there.

How the folder turns into playlists:

In the folderIn OCTAVE
A sub-folderA playlist with the folder's name. An empty folder is an empty playlist.
Songs directly in the folderThe playlist Unsorted.
EverythingThe playlist All Music, which holds every song of every folder.
Folders inside a sub-folderNot read. OCTAVE looks one level down only.
Folders whose name starts with a dotSkipped.

File types

OCTAVE lists files ending in .mp3, .m4a, .mp4 (audio-only), .flac, .ogg, .opus and .wav. Whether a file then plays depends on the codecs your system gives Qt. Covers are read from inside the file for every type except WAV. A file with no tags is shown by its file name, with "Unknown Artist" and "Unknown Album".

Playing Music

The music button in the bottom bar opens the music section. It has two screens, and tapping the button again switches between them:

  • The library: the list of songs in one playlist.
  • The Media Room: the song that is playing, large, with its cover and controls.

In the library:

  • The PLAYLIST box at the top right picks which playlist is shown.
  • The top left shows what the playlist holds: songs, albums, artists and total playing time.
  • Tap a song to play it. OCTAVE then opens the Media Room, unless "Return to Library After Picking a Song" is on.
  • Tap TITLE, ARTIST or ALBUM above the list to sort by that column; tap again to reverse it. On an upright screen the album column is left out.
  • Press and hold a song for its actions: Move to playlist and Delete song.

Play, pause, next, previous and shuffle are in the bottom bar on every page, and on the wireless knob and the gesture sensor if you have them. At the end of a playlist OCTAVE starts again from the top. There is no repeat-one mode. With shuffle on, the song that is playing carries on and the rest of the playlist follows in a random order.

The title shown for a song is its file name, tidied: the artist's name, track numbers and leftovers such as "(Official Audio)" are removed. Artist and album come from the file's tags. The files themselves are never renamed. Clean Titles in Settings > Media tidies every title again.

Playlists

A playlist is a folder, so everything you do to a playlist in OCTAVE happens to the folder on disk, and anything you do to the folders with a file manager shows up in OCTAVE after a Scan.

All Music and Unsorted cannot be deleted, and a song cannot be moved to All Music (it is not a folder). If the song being moved or deleted is the one playing, playback stops first.

The Media Room

The Media Room shows what is playing, whichever source it comes from: your files, Spotify, a mirrored phone or Android Auto. On it are the cover, the title, artist and album, previous / play / next, a volume slider with a mute button, the seek bar, shuffle, and a visualizer that moves with the music.

  • Tap the cover to choose what that corner shows: Album art, Vinyl, a 3D record player, MP3 player, CD player, cassette player or iPod, or (where phone mirroring can run) Phone mirror, which puts the phone's live, touchable screen there. The choice needs Qt's 3D modules; without them a tap does nothing.
  • The customize button at the top right opens the look editor. See Pages.
  • Shuffle is dimmed while a phone app other than Spotify has the music: that app's shuffle is its own.

The Album Art Capture theme recolours the whole app from the cover of the song that is playing.

Spotify

Spotify support is experimental. OCTAVE does not play Spotify's audio itself: the Spotify app on your phone (or any other Spotify device) plays, and OCTAVE is its remote. Controlling playback needs Spotify Premium.

Setting it up

Spotify only lets an app sign in the accounts its owner has listed, so each OCTAVE user registers their own free "app" with Spotify once. A build made with -DOCTAVE_SPOTIFY_CLIENT_ID=<id> already has one, and skips steps 1 to 3.

  1. At developer.spotify.com, create an app that uses the Web API.
  2. Open Settings > Media > Spotify in OCTAVE. Copy the Redirect URI shown there into the Spotify app's settings (http://127.0.0.1:43821/callback on a computer or head unit; the page's own address in the browser demo). Add your Spotify account under User Management.
  3. Paste the app's Client ID (32 letters and digits) into the Client ID field.
  4. Tap Connect Spotify and finish signing in in the browser that opens. The card then says "Signed in as" and your name.

The Connect Music sheet's Spotify row and the "Set Up Spotify…" / "Connect Spotify…" entry in the PLAYLIST box both lead to this card. In the browser demo the Client ID can also be given in the address, ?spotify=<Client ID>, because pasting into a field is unreliable on a phone.

Using it

  • The PLAYLIST box gains a Spotify heading under your own playlists, with Liked Songs, your playlists and your saved albums. Pick one and its songs fill the same list.
  • Tap a song to play it. Once Spotify has accepted, the Media Room, the bottom bar, the knob and gestures all control Spotify, shuffle included.
  • The button to the left of the PLAYLIST box names the device Spotify is playing on (or says Choose device). Tap it to move playback to another device. If nothing is listed, open Spotify on your phone.
  • If no device is active when you press play, OCTAVE wakes one: the active one if any, else a phone, else the first in the list.
  • OCTAVE's volume and mute set the Spotify device's own volume. Some phones do not allow that; OCTAVE says so and leaves them alone.
  • Closing OCTAVE pauses Spotify, so the phone does not play on with nothing to stop it. A power cut cannot do that.

Limits

  • Playback control needs Spotify Premium.
  • Spotify gives the song list only for playlists you own or collaborate on. A playlist you merely follow shows "Spotify doesn't share the songs of playlists you follow" with a Play button that plays it from the top.
  • Liked Songs lists at most 1000 songs.
  • No sound reaches OCTAVE, so the visualizer stays flat.
  • A command that fails (no device, no Premium, no network) shows a notice with the reason on whichever page you are, and the music that was playing is left alone.
  • Not available in the frontend playground of the browser demo.

While you mirror a phone that is itself running Spotify, the phone keeps the controls and nothing is paused when OCTAVE switches between the two: they are two remotes for the same player.

Volume

One volume, shown as 0 to 100 %, sets every output at once: your music, phone audio, Android Auto, AirPlay, Spotify's device and the knob's light ring. Raising the volume while muted unmutes, and pressing mute at 0 % brings back the startup volume.

Rule: every volume change goes through volumeController
Screens, hardware handlers and service clients call volumeController.applyVolume(percent) and volumeController.toggleMute(), and show percent, muted and effectivelyMuted. Never call a manager's own setVolume / setMuted, and never do the percent-to-loudness maths yourself: an output set on the side drifts away from the slider. The curve and the routing are described under Architecture.

When a mirrored phone or Android Auto speaks (a navigation prompt, a call), your music is turned down for as long as it speaks and comes back up afterwards. This "ducking" is separate from the volume: the slider does not move. Its settings are on the Phone Mirror page.

How It Works

From here on the page is for people who want to know what happens underneath, or who are changing the code.

Several things can make music in OCTAVE, but only one at a time, and every screen should look and behave the same whichever it is. So the screens do not talk to the players. They talk to one object, nowPlaying, which knows which source has the music and passes the controls on to it.

PieceJob
mediaManagerThe local library and player: reads the folder, builds the playlists, reads tags and covers, and plays files through Qt's QMediaPlayer.
spotifyManagerSpotify over its Web API: sign-in, your library, and remote control of the device that plays.
nowPlayingWhat is playing right now and its controls, whichever source has them. Its source is "local", "phone", "androidauto" or "spotify".
volumeControllerThe one volume and the one mute, sent to every output.

The library screen is the exception: it lists and edits local files, so it calls mediaManager directly (and spotifyManager for a Spotify list). All four objects are context properties in QML and service objects in the service API under the same names.

How the Library Is Read

  1. List the folder. The scan lists the library folder and each sub-folder, by file extension only. No file is opened, so it is quick. It runs at startup, when the folder setting changes, on Scan, and after every playlist edit.
  2. Build the playlists. "All Music" first, "Unsorted" second, then the folders in alphabetical order. If two folders hold a file of the same name, the second appears in All Music as "folder - file".
  3. Tidy the titles of songs that have none yet.
  4. Keep your place. The playlist and song that were selected stay selected.
  5. Read the tags in the background, for the selected playlist (next section).

The scan reports what it finds line by line through the scanProgress signal; the Scan Log at the bottom of Settings > Media > Library & Playback shows those lines after a Scan or Clean Titles.

Nothing outside the library folder
Songs are named by file name, never by a path the caller chooses. A name with .. in it or a leading slash is refused, and every create, move and delete checks that the result is inside the library folder. This matters because the same calls are open to paired devices over the service API.

Tags and Covers

Reading a song's tags means opening the file, and a library has thousands. On a head unit's SD card that is slow, so OCTAVE does it once and remembers the answer.

  • The tag store. Title, artist, album and length of every song read so far are kept in metadata_cache.json in the config folder. An entry is used as long as the file's size and modification time are unchanged; otherwise the file is read again.
  • Background reading. When a playlist is selected, a worker thread goes through it, taking each song from the store or reading the file. Until it is done, rows show placeholders and the song / album / artist counts show the previous values; the screens fill in when totalDurationChanged, albumCountChanged and artistCountChanged arrive. A view that sorts by artist or album should sort again then.
  • It survives being cut off. The store is written every 30 seconds while a pass runs, so a library too big to read in one drive carries on where it stopped.
  • Titles. The tidied titles are kept in display_names.json beside it.

Covers

mediaManager.getAlbumArt(file) returns a file:// address of the song's cover, or an empty string if it has none. The picture is taken from inside the audio file the first time and saved as cover_<hash> in a temp folder (beside the program when that is writable, in the user's app-data folder for an installed build and on Android). The hash is made from the album and artist, so every song of an album shares one cover file and a cover from an earlier run is found without opening the audio file. A song with no album tag gets a cover of its own. At most 500 covers are kept; the least recently used are deleted.

After each track change the tags and covers of the three songs before and after it are fetched in the background, so the Media Room's side cards and the next skip are instant.

Resuming After a Restart

A car stops by cutting the power, not by closing the app. So the song, the playlist and the position are saved a second after every skip or pause, and every 10 seconds while a song plays. After a power cut you are at most 10 seconds from where you were.

At startup the last playlist is selected and the last song loaded at its position, paused. It plays by itself only with Autoplay on Startup on. Shuffle comes back only with Remember Shuffle on. If the song is no longer in the playlist, nothing is loaded.

Files That Will Not Play

Some files cannot be played: a file that was cut short, a file with tags and no audio, a file on a USB stick that was pulled out. OCTAVE never gets stuck on one.

  • A file that stalls or fails is reloaded at the same position, twice. Then it is skipped.
  • A file that is missing is skipped at once.
  • After 10 bad files in a row (or the whole playlist, if it is shorter) playback stops, so a broken library is not cycled forever.
  • Resuming after a pause of 30 seconds or more reloads the song at its position first. On some audio systems a long-paused player reports "playing" and makes no sound; a fresh load always works.

Each skip and each give-up is written to the log with the file's name.

Which Source Has the Music

nowPlaying decides who owns the music. Only something the user did changes the owner:

  • Playing a local song takes the music. A phone or Spotify that was playing is paused.
  • The mirrored phone takes it when it takes the media controls.
  • Android Auto asks, and gets it only after a user action aimed at it.
  • Spotify takes it when you play something from OCTAVE and Spotify accepts, or when it starts by itself while nothing else is playing. It never takes over music that another source is playing.

A phone and Spotify report their position only now and then, so nowPlaying counts it forward in between. Their covers arrive a moment after the track does, so the previous cover is held for up to 1.5 seconds and no "missing art" picture flashes during a track change.

How Spotify Is Connected

  • Sign-in is OAuth with PKCE, which needs no secret in the program. On a computer OCTAVE opens the system browser and waits for Spotify's answer on 127.0.0.1:43821. In the browser demo the page goes to Spotify and reads the answer when it comes back.
  • The session is kept in spotify.json in the config folder (the browser's storage in the demo). Only Spotify revoking it signs OCTAVE out.
  • The player's state is polled: every 2 seconds while playing, every 6 while idle. One empty answer is ignored, because Spotify gives one between tracks.
  • No network is normal in a car. Requests fail quietly, polling carries on, and the first poll that works fetches the library that did not come at startup.
  • Volume is sent as OCTAVE's plain percent (the device has its own curve), at most about three times a second while a slider is dragged. Mute sends 0 %.

When Something Goes Wrong

  1. "No Music Loaded". Check the folder in Settings > Media > Library & Playback, tap Scan and read the Scan Log: it names the path and every playlist found. Remember that only the folder and one level of sub-folders are read. A red line under the folder field means the folder could not be used.
  2. A song is skipped or will not play. The file is damaged or in a codec your system lacks. The log names it ("Giving up on ...").
  3. Wrong artist or album. OCTAVE shows the file's tags. Fix the tags with a tag editor; the changed file is read again by itself. metadata_cache.json is only a cache and can be deleted.
  4. Odd titles. Tap Clean Titles.
  5. No sound. Check Settings > Media > Audio Output, which says where OCTAVE is playing.
  6. Spotify. The Spotify card in Settings > Media shows the last problem in red, in plain words (wrong Redirect URI, account not under User Management, Premium needed).
  7. The log files. The categories are octave.media, octave.nowplaying and octave.spotify. See Architecture for where the logs are.

More problems and fixes are on the Troubleshooting page.

Why It Is Built This Way

  • Folders are the playlists. There is no playlist database to get out of step with the disk. Every edit is a plain file operation followed by a scan, and you can arrange your music with any file manager.
  • One object for "what is playing". Screens bind to nowPlaying and never ask which source is live. Adding a source (Spotify was the latest) changes no screen.
  • One source owns the music, and only the user changes the owner. Nothing starts playing over something else by itself.
  • One volume, one place. When each output did its own maths, they drifted apart whenever one changed. Volume, mute and ducking are also kept as three separate values, so a muted or ducked level is never mistaken for the volume the user chose.
  • Tags are read once and kept on disk. Tags rarely change, and size plus modification time is a cheap and sufficient check. Opening every file on every start made the head unit crawl.
  • Covers are named by a hash of the album. The file name can be worked out without opening the audio file, so covers survive restarts.
  • File reading stays off the main thread. Tags, covers and the Album Art Capture colours are worked out on worker threads and handed back; only the main thread touches the caches, so no lock is needed and list scrolling never waits on a disk.
  • Save often, in small writes. Power cuts are the normal way a car stops. Position every 10 seconds, the tag store every 30, each written atomically so a cut leaves the old file or the new one.
  • Give up on bad files. Two tries, then skip; ten skips, then stop. "Recovered" is only claimed when the player has really loaded the file.
  • Spotify is a remote, not a player. The Web API controls the Spotify app and hands out no audio. That is why the visualizer is flat and why a Spotify device must be on.
