What They Do

OCTAVE can use three optional pieces of hardware. None is needed to run the app, and each one is found and connected without you doing anything once it is wired up.

DeviceWhat it gives you in the car
Volume knobA real knob for the volume: turn it for louder or quieter, press it to mute. A ring of LEDs around it shows the level.
Motion sensor (BerryIMU v3)Pitch, roll, compass heading, G-force, altitude and cabin temperature, as gauges and as a 3D model of the vehicle that tilts as the vehicle does.
Gesture sensor (PAJ7620U2)Wave a hand in front of it to skip a track, change the volume or pause, without touching the screen.

Volume Knob

The knob is two small ESP32 boards. One sits where your hand can reach it and holds the knob. The other, an ESP32-S3 "receiver", plugs into a USB port of the computer that runs OCTAVE. The two talk to each other by radio, so no cable runs to the knob. OCTAVE only ever talks to the receiver.

  • Turn the knob: the volume goes up or down by the amount set under Change per Click.
  • Press it: mute, and press again to unmute.
  • The LED ring shows the volume, whether sound is muted, and a colour: the colour of the album art that is playing, or one you pick.

The firmware for the two boards is not in the OCTAVE repository. Any firmware that speaks the serial protocol below works.

Connecting it

  1. Plug the receiver into USB before starting OCTAVE. Three seconds after it starts, OCTAVE looks at the serial ports. If you have not chosen a port and one of them is an ESP32-S3, OCTAVE saves that port and connects.
  2. If that did not happen, open Settings > Accessories > Volume Knob. Under USB Serial Port there is a chip for each port; an ESP32-S3 has a star. Tap yours. Refresh looks for ports again.
  3. If you picked a port OCTAVE does not recognise as an ESP32-S3, the page says "turn the knob to confirm". Turn or press the knob once. Until then OCTAVE only listens on that port and sends it nothing.

The line under Connection tells you where things stand:

Motion Sensor (BerryIMU)

An IMU (inertial measurement unit) is a small board that feels how it is being moved and tilted. The BerryIMU v3 holds an accelerometer, a gyroscope, a magnetometer (compass) and a barometer. Fixed to the vehicle, it tells OCTAVE how the vehicle is leaning, which way it points, how hard it is cornering or braking, and how high it is.

The sensor button on the bottom bar opens a page with two tiles, Sensors and 3D View. Where the readings show up:

WhereWhat you see
SensorsCards for pitch, roll, heading, G-force, altitude and temperature. Choose which under Visible Cards in the settings.
3D ViewA 3D model of the vehicle that pitches and rolls as the vehicle does, with the heading above it. With no sensor the model sits level and the page says "No motion sensor". See Pages.
Home and dashboardsThe readings are ordinary gauge parameters, so a compass, a G-force gauge or a pitch readout can go on any dashboard. See Gauges & Dashboards.

The readings, by the id you use in a dashboard:

Setting level

The board is rarely mounted perfectly flat. Park on level ground and tap ZERO on the Sensors page, or Set level here in the 3D View's menu. From then on pitch and roll read 0 in that position, and the two G-force readings read 0 too. RESET (Reset level) undoes it.

  • Level is not saved. Set it again after restarting OCTAVE.
  • Keep the vehicle still for the first second after the sensor connects. That is when OCTAVE measures the gyroscope's resting error, and movement then makes the readings drift.

One Volume for Everything

The knob, the gesture sensor and the sliders on the screen all change the same volume, and they all follow the same rules:

  • The volume is one number from 0 to 100. It is applied to local music, the mirrored phone, Android Auto, AirPlay and, while Spotify is playing, to Spotify. The knob's LED ring shows it.
  • Turning the volume up while muted unmutes, whichever input did it.
  • Mute at 0 % does not mute. It brings the sound back at the Startup Volume from Settings > Media.
  • Mute is not saved, and neither is the volume: OCTAVE starts unmuted at the Startup Volume.
  • Five mute presses within 4 seconds take you back to OCTAVE's built-in screen when an installed frontend has taken over the display. It works from the knob, a gesture or the screen.

All of this lives in one object, volumeController. Anything that changes the volume calls volumeController.applyVolume(percent) or toggleMute(), and anything that shows it binds to percent, muted and effectivelyMuted (muted, or at 0 %). Ears do not hear loudness in a straight line, so the percent is squared on its way to the audio outputs: 50 % on the slider is a quarter of full power. toLinear(percent) and toPercent(linear) give that curve to code that needs it; nothing else should do the sum itself.

How It Works

From here on the page is for people who want to know what happens underneath, or who are changing the code.

Each device has a manager: a C++ object that owns the connection, turns what the device says into Qt signals, and is visible to the screens and to the service API under one name.

The managers do not know about each other. src/appcore.cpp connects them: a knob turn or a volume gesture becomes applyVolume(), a track gesture becomes a call on nowPlaying, and each motion reading goes into paramStore, the store every gauge reads from.

On Android and in the browser the knob and gesture managers are empty stand-ins with the same names, properties and methods, so the screens load unchanged. They never connect and never emit anything.

The Knob: Serial Protocol

The receiver and OCTAVE exchange short lines of text over USB serial at 115200 baud, 8 data bits, no parity, one stop bit. Every line ends in a newline. Upper and lower case are the same.

DirectionLineMeaning
Knob to OCTAVE+ or -One click up or down.
+3, -2Several clicks at once.
MThe knob was pressed: toggle mute.
PReserved for play / pause. Accepted, and does nothing yet.
OCTAVE to knobV42The volume is 42 %: light the ring to match.
M1, M0Muted, not muted.
C255,128,0The ring's colour, as red, green, blue.
  • Each click is worth Change per Click, which can be a fraction of a percent. The volume is a whole number, so fractions are added up and applied once they reach 1 %.
  • Every press of the knob is answered with M0 or M1, even a press that changed nothing, so the knob's own light can never disagree with OCTAVE. Presses less than 150 ms apart count as one: the button bounces.
  • A line OCTAVE does not know is ignored. More than 256 bytes with no newline is treated as noise and thrown away up to the next newline.
  • The protocol has no "who are you" question. That is why a port that does not look like an ESP32-S3 has to prove itself with a knob turn.

Finding and trusting the port

  1. Three seconds after startup the manager lists the serial ports, leaving out the one saved for the OBD adapter so the two never fight over a device.
  2. A port counts as an ESP32-S3 if its USB vendor id is Espressif's (0x303A) or its description contains "jtag", "esp32-s3" or "esp32s3".
  3. If no port is saved and an ESP32-S3 is there, it is saved as esp32VolumePort. No other kind of port is ever chosen automatically: it could be unrelated hardware.
  4. The saved port is opened. An ESP32-S3 is trusted at once (status Connected). Any other port is only listened to (status Waiting) until a valid knob line arrives.
  5. Half a second after Connected, OCTAVE sends the volume, the mute state and the colour, so a freshly plugged knob matches the app. The delay lets the receiver finish starting.

The list of ports is esp32VolumeManager.availablePorts: one {port, label, description, isEsp32S3} per port, ESP32-S3 first. refreshPorts() rescans; the settings page calls it when it opens and from its Refresh chip.

LED ring

  • Volume. Every volume change is sent as V, but at most 20 times a second, and the last value always goes out. That keeps the ring from flickering while a slider is dragged.
  • Sleep. The receiver is expected to turn its LEDs off after 5 seconds without hearing from OCTAVE. With Keep LEDs on, OCTAVE repeats the current volume every 2 seconds to keep them lit. With it off (the default) OCTAVE sends nothing extra and the ring sleeps by itself. It also goes dark when OCTAVE exits.
  • Colour. In Theme Colors mode the ring gets the accent colour taken from the album art, each time the art changes. In Static Color mode it gets esp32LedStaticColor. Changing mode sends the right colour at once. A colour that is missing or cannot be read becomes cyan.
  • Mute. Sent whenever mute changes, from any input.

Losing and regaining the knob

The link counts as lost when the serial port reports that the device is gone or can no longer be read or written, or when a keep-alive line cannot be written. The manager then tries to open the port again after 3, 6, 9, 10 and 10 seconds. After five failures it stops retrying, shows "Max retries reached" and waits for the receiver to appear.

  • Tapping the port in Settings starts over with a fresh count of five.
  • With esp32AutoReconnect off there are no retries at all, and a receiver that appears is not connected: only startup and a tap in Settings connect.
  • While the knob is on and not connected, the manager rescans the ports every 10 seconds. When the saved port appears (it was not there at the last scan) it connects. When no port is saved, or the saved one is gone and an ESP32-S3 appears under another name, it saves that port and connects. So a receiver plugged in after startup, or back after the retries ran out, is working within 10 seconds. A port that is not an ESP32-S3 is never picked by itself.

The Motion Sensor: Chips

The BerryIMU v3 is three chips on one I2C bus. The bus number and the addresses are fixed in the code.

ChipAddressMeasuresSet up as
LSM6DSL0x6AAcceleration and rotation rate416 samples a second, ±4 g, 245 degrees a second
LIS3MDL0x1CMagnetic field (compass)155 samples a second, ±8 gauss
BMP3880x77Air pressure and temperatureRead 5 times a second

The gesture sensor is a fourth device on the same bus, at 0x73. Both managers open /dev/i2c-2 themselves and talk to the chips directly; no kernel driver or extra library is involved.

The read loop

Reading the three chips blocks for a few milliseconds each time, which is far too long for the thread that draws the screen. So a worker object on its own thread does all of it and hands finished values to the main thread as signals.

  1. Open the bus and set up the three chips. Status becomes Connected.
  2. Measure the gyroscope's resting error: 200 readings over one second, averaged. This is subtracted from every later reading. It is measured at every start because it changes with temperature.
  3. Then, about 200 times a second: read acceleration, rotation and magnetic field; update the attitude; if it is time, send values to the screen; five times a second also read the barometer; sleep for whatever is left of the 5 ms.

On the Orange Pi 5 Plus the three reads take about 4.3 ms, so the loop really runs at about 172 times a second and its thread uses about 8 % of one core. That is the cost of reading the sensor. The 5 ms period must not be set below the time the reads take, or the loop never sleeps.

From readings to attitude

A gyroscope is precise over a moment and drifts over a minute. An accelerometer knows where "down" is in the long run and is noisy in the short run. A Madgwick filter combines the two into one steady answer for how the board is tilted. The answer is a quaternion: four numbers that describe a rotation and, unlike three angles, never get stuck when the vehicle points straight up or down.

  • The compass is kept out of the filter. A car is full of magnets and steel (alternator, speakers, body), and letting the magnetometer steer the filter would pull the tilt around. The magnetometer is used for one thing only: HEADING, straight from its two horizontal axes.
  • The turn to left or right is removed from the quaternion before it goes out. The 3D model needs only pitch and roll, and compass drift must never spin it.
  • Level is applied last. ZERO stores the tilt at that moment, and every later tilt is given relative to it. It stores the two horizontal accelerations as well, which is what makes the G-force readings 0.
  • Pitch and roll are taken from that same final quaternion, so the numbers on screen always agree with the model.
  • A still vehicle does not move the model. Rotation below 0.4 degrees a second counts as none, and while there is none the filter is not run.

The result leaves the manager as orientationChanged(w, x, y, z) for the 3D View, and as one signal per reading (pitchChanged, rollChanged, headingChanged and so on). src/appcore.cpp feeds those into paramStore under the ids in the table near the top of this page. To show a reading in QML, use OBDParameterModel.liveValue("PITCH"); see Gauges & Dashboards.

How often values reach the screen

The loop always reads and filters at full speed, because the filter needs every sample. What changes is how often the results are sent to the screen, since each send wakes up everything that shows a reading.

  • While a page shows motion readings: at the Update Rate setting, 60 times a second by default.
  • While none does: 5 times a second.
  • Altitude and temperature: always 5 times a second.

A page says it shows motion readings by putting an ImuDemand in itself. It is the motion sensor's version of obdManager.setParameterDemand: 

App.ImuDemand { consumer: "myPage"; wanted: myPage.visible }

That calls berryIMU.setDemand(consumer, wanted). The sensor runs at the full rate while at least one consumer wants it. The Sensors pages, the 3D View, Live Data, and Home when it shows a motion reading all do this. A page you add needs its own ImuDemand, or its readings update only 5 times a second. A program using the service API calls setDemand itself; the request is taken back when its connection closes.

Starting, failing and retrying

The motion sensor and the gesture sensor behave the same way here.

  • The first try is one second after OCTAVE starts, and only if the sensor's setting is on.
  • The bus or a chip is missing: the manager tries again after 5, 10, 15, 20 and 25 seconds, then gives up until you turn the sensor off and on in Settings or restart OCTAVE.
  • Permission denied on /dev/i2c-2: no retry, because waiting will not fix it. The log says to add the user to the i2c group.
  • A read fails while running: one failure is skipped. Five in a row count as a dead bus: status Error, then the retries above.
  • Turning the setting off stops the thread and closes the bus. Turning it on starts again with a fresh count.
  • On Windows and macOS there is no I2C bus: the status is Disconnected and nothing is retried.

With the simulator (octave --sim, and the browser demo's simulated car) the real sensor thread is stopped and the same signals carry made-up motion that follows the simulated drive. The screens cannot tell the difference.

Android

A phone has these sensors built in, so on Android berryIMU is a different implementation behind the same name, signals and methods. It reads the phone's accelerometer, compass and pressure sensor.

  • Pitch and roll come from the accelerometer alone, with no gyroscope and no filter.
  • Setting level stores the two angles and subtracts them. It does not zero the G-force readings.
  • While no page shows motion readings the phone's sensors are stopped, not slowed down, to save battery.
  • Most phones do not report a temperature. The temperature card is hidden until one arrives (berryIMU.hasTemperature).
  • The status is Connected, Unavailable (the phone has no accelerometer) or Disabled.

The Gesture Sensor: Reading Gestures

The PAJ7620U2 does the recognising itself. It raises a flag for each gesture it sees, and clears the flags when they are read. OCTAVE's part is to ask often, and to not be fooled by a gesture that is still forming.

At startup the worker wakes the chip, checks that it identifies itself as a PAJ7620 (id 0x7620), and loads a fixed table of settings (the DFRobot reference configuration). Then it reads the flags 50 times a second:

  1. No flag: ask again in 20 ms.
  2. A flag during the Pause Between Gestures after the last gesture: ignored.
  3. Towards or away. A swipe often begins by looking like a hand coming closer. So the worker waits (0.3 s after "towards", 0.4 s after "away") and reads again. If the sensor now reports a swipe, circle or wave, that is the gesture. Otherwise it really was towards or away.
  4. Anything else is read once more 20 ms later. The same gesture or nothing confirms it. A different gesture means the sensor has not settled, and the reading is dropped.
  5. The gesture is announced (gestureDetected). If it is mapped to an action other than Disabled, the action is announced too (actionTriggered).
  6. For the next 0.1 s the flags are read and thrown away, so the tail of the same movement cannot fire again.

actionTriggered carries one of next_track, previous_track, play_pause, mute_toggle, volume_up, volume_down. src/appcore.cpp carries them out: the first three on nowPlaying, the rest on volumeController. The gestures, the actions with their labels and the default mapping are one table in src/managers/gesturevocabulary.h; the settings page lists gestureSensor.gestures and gestureSensor.actions from it.

When Something Goes Wrong

  1. Settings > Accessories. Each tile says Connected, Not found or Off. The knob's own page gives the reason in words.
  2. The log. Each manager writes what it found and why it gave up to octave-cpp.log, under the categories octave.esp32volume, octave.berryimu (octave.imu.mobile on Android) and octave.gesture. See Architecture for where the logs are. Lines worth looking for:
    • detected ESP32-S3 on …: the receiver was recognised.
    • permission denied on /dev/i2c-2 - add the user to the i2c group; not retrying
    • LSM6DSL init failed (or LIS3MDL, BMP388): the bus opened but that chip did not answer. Check the wiring.
    • expected chip ID 0x7620, got …: something else is at the gesture sensor's address.
    • max retries (5) reached, giving up
    • RIGHT -> next_track (#12): a gesture was seen and acted on.
  3. The bus itself. Hardware Setup shows how to check that the chips answer on the I2C bus, outside OCTAVE.

Common problems and their fixes are on the Troubleshooting page.

Why It Is Built This Way

  • The I2C sensors have their own threads; the knob does not. A round of motion readings blocks for milliseconds and the gesture loop waits up to 0.4 s on purpose. Neither may happen on the thread that draws the screen. The knob sends a few bytes a second, so the serial port's notifications on the main thread are enough.
  • Slow the sending, not the sensor. Stopping the loop while no page is open would lose the attitude and the level setting, and the first frame after opening the 3D View would be wrong. Sending 5 times a second costs almost nothing and keeps everything current.
  • Pages ask for the full rate themselves. What needs fast readings is a matter of what is on screen, and only the page knows that. A central list of page names went stale every time a page was added.
  • Level is a button, not a mounting-angle setting. Nobody knows the angle their board is mounted at. Everybody can park on flat ground and tap ZERO.
  • One entry point for volume. Every output must use the same curve and the same mute rules, or they drift apart as soon as two inputs are used. The outputs follow a signal from volumeController, so it needs to know none of them.
  • LED sleep is the absence of a message. The receiver sleeps when it hears nothing, so it needs no "sleep" command and goes dark by itself when OCTAVE exits or crashes.
  • Stand-ins on platforms without the hardware. The same names exist everywhere, so no screen needs to ask which platform it is on.
