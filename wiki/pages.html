What a Page Is

OCTAVE shows one screen at a time above (or beside) the bottom bar: Home, the music, the car's readings, the sensors, Settings. Each of those screens is a page. This is the tour of all of them.

Each section below says what the page is for and what you can do on it, then, under "How it is built", which file it is and what a developer should know before changing it. The last two sections are for developers only.

Home

Home is the screen OCTAVE starts on. What it looks like is your choice: Settings > Display > Layout > Home Screen shows a card with a picture for each Home, and tapping one picks it. There are three kinds.

The dashboard (the default)

Out of the box Home is a dashboard: your phone's screen in the middle, a Now Playing card under it, speed and RPM on the left, coolant temperature and battery voltage on the right. In an upright window the phone is on top, the four readings sit in a row under it and the music card runs along the bottom.

  • The phone. While a phone is mirrored, its screen is live here and your touches go to the phone. Otherwise the card says "No phone connected" with a Connect Phone button, or Set Up, which takes you to Settings > Phone. See Phone Mirror & Android Auto.
  • The music card. Tap it to open the music (the Media Room or the library, as the Media button does). Its progress bar seeks without leaving Home. With no music it shows "No Music Loaded" and a Connect Music button, which offers Local Music, Spotify and Phone.
  • The readings. With no car connected they are covered by "No vehicle connected" and a Connect Vehicle button. See OBD Manager.
  • A widget that shows the motion sensor reads "No sensor" until one is connected.

Only the music card opens a page when tapped. The other widgets do nothing on a tap for now.

Any dashboard can be Home. A dashboard is a JSON file you write and put in the dashboards folder of OCTAVE's settings folder; it then appears among the Home Screen cards. There is no dashboard editor in the app. Gauges & Dashboards explains how to write one.

Hand-built Home screens

Six Homes are drawn by hand and follow no grid. Each shows a Connect Vehicle button while no car is connected.

Classic

The Classic card is the three-panel Home: music on the left, the car's readings in the middle with a picture of your vehicle, the motion sensor on the right. Upright, the three stack. Each panel opens its section when tapped: the music panel opens the music, the readings open Live Data, the vehicle picture opens Settings > Device > Vehicle, and the sensor panel opens the sensors hub. With nothing connected, the panels show the same "No vehicle connected", "No Music Loaded" and "No sensors connected" prompts.

How it is built

frontend/MainMenu.qml is the page. It reads the homeScreen setting and loads one of three things:

homeScreenWhat is loaded
"" (default)frontend/HomeDashboard.qml: the dashboard dashboardManager.homeDashboardId names, drawn by the same DashboardRenderer as any dashboard. Until you pick another, that is the built-in dashboards/presets/home.json.
cockpit, glass, trail, retro, rings, telemetryThe matching file in frontend/home/, looked up in home/HomeScreens.js.
classicThe three panels in MainMenu itself, using HomeOBDView, HomeVehicleCard and HomeSensorPanel.

Taps on the dashboard. The renderer reports a tap as an action (media, obd, sensors, vehicle). HomeDashboard.tapActionsThatOpen lists the actions that open a page; it holds only "media". Adding an action to that list is all it takes to switch another one on.

Prompts. HomeDashboard lays the prompts over the widgets rather than leaving them to read "--". The vehicle prompt covers the block of OBD widgets when nothing else stands in that block, and becomes a strip along the bottom when something does. The music prompt is frontend/MusicEmptyPrompt.qml, the same one the Media Room and the library show. Its Phone choice starts mirroring and goes Home, where the phone is shown; for an iPhone it opens the AirPlay page.

Polling. Home asks the car only for what it shows: the dashboard registers its widgets' readings with obdManager.setParameterDemand("homeDashboard", ids) while it is on screen, and asks for the motion sensor's full rate only when a widget uses it. A hand-built Home does the same through its base type.

Adding a hand-built Home. Each one is a HomeScreen (home/HomeScreen.qml), which hands it the readings by id (with "--" when there is none), what is playing, whether the window is upright (portrait) and a unit u that is 1 % of the window's short side, so the screen scales with its room. To add one:

  1. Write the file in frontend/home/.
  2. Add a row to home/HomeScreens.js with its preview, a 640 × 310 picture in frontend/assets/home/.
  3. Add a row to homeScreens_data in tst_dashboardwidgets, which loads every Home.
  4. Look at it in both orientations.

Media Room

The Media Room is the "now playing" screen: the album art, large, over a blurred copy of itself, with the title, artist and album, a seek bar, Previous / Play / Next, Shuffle, the volume slider with its mute button, and bars that move with the music. It shows whatever is playing, whether that is a file from your library, Spotify or the phone. There is no repeat button.

What you can do here beyond the transport buttons:

  • Change what stands in the art's place. Tap the album art. A ring of choices opens: Album art, Vinyl (the art as a spinning record), five 3D players (Record player, MP3 player, CD player, Cassette player, iPod) and, where phone mirroring can run, Phone mirror. Turn the ring by dragging, tap an item to bring it to the front, and tap the front one to pick it. Tap beside the ring to close it. The ring needs the Qt Quick 3D module; without it a tap on the art does nothing.
  • Use the phone here. With Phone mirror picked, the phone's screen takes the art's place, live and touchable, with Connect Phone when nothing is mirrored. Every tap on the picture goes to the phone, so two small buttons sit just off it: three dots at the top right open the ring again, and a ring at the top left, shown while a phone is mirrored, is the phone's Home key.
  • Change the look. The customize button at the top right opens the look editor.
  • Add music. With no music the page shows "No Music Loaded" and Connect Music. When songs first appear in an empty library, the first one starts playing.

Shuffle is dimmed while a phone app other than Spotify has the music: its shuffle belongs to that app.

How it is built

frontend/MediaRoom.qml. Everything about the playing track comes from nowPlaying, which follows the phone while it has the music and local playback otherwise, so the page never asks which source is playing. The controls call nowPlaying too, and volume goes through volumeController.

Full lists of what the page can call: nowPlaying, mediaManager, audioAnalyzer.

The small page in the corner is the real Media Room scaled down, not a copy, with one touch area over it so nothing on it reacts but the tap that closes the editor. Leaving the Media Room closes the editor.

Music Library

The library is the list of your songs. The Media button switches between it and the Media Room. Each row shows a song's art, title, artist, album and length; the bar at the top counts the songs, albums and artists and their total length.

To do thisDo this
Play a songTap its row. The Media Room opens, unless Settings > Media > Return to Library After Picking a Song is on.
SortTap the TITLE, ARTIST or ALBUM heading; tap again to reverse.
Show one playlistTap PLAYLIST and pick one. A playlist is a folder inside your music folder.
Make a playlistTap +, type a name (the button in the name field opens an on-screen keyboard) and tap Create.
Move or delete a songPress and hold its row, then Move to playlist or Delete song. Delete removes the file for good and asks first.
Delete a playlistPress and hold it in the playlist list. This deletes the songs in it. "All Music" and "Unsorted" cannot be deleted.

Spotify. Under the local playlists the list has a Spotify heading. Signed out, it holds Connect Spotify…, or Set Up Spotify… when no Client ID has been entered yet. Signed in, it lists your Spotify library, and a pick shows its songs in the same table. Those cannot be sorted: the order is Spotify's. A button names the device Spotify is playing on and opens the list of devices to move playback to. See Media Manager: Spotify.

The phone's queue. While a mirrored phone has the music and reports a queue, the list can show that queue in the same way.

With no songs the page shows the same "No Music Loaded" prompt as Home. There is no search and no drag to reorder.

How it is built

frontend/MediaPlayer.qml. Local songs and playlists come from mediaManager; Spotify's from spotifyManager. The list scrolls to the playing song when the page opens and follows it on a track change, unless you are scrolling. A failed move or delete shows the reason in the sheet instead of closing it. Full lists: mediaManager, spotifyManager.

OBD

The OBD page is the way in to everything about the car. With no adapter connected it shows only "No vehicle connected" and a Connect Vehicle button, which opens the sheet that finds and connects an adapter (OBD Manager). Once connected it shows three cards:

CardUnder itOpens
Live DataEvery reading from the carLive Data
Trouble CodesRead & clear DTCsTrouble Codes
Trip History"Recording this drive", "n drives logged", "Black box drive logs", or "Black box unavailable"Trip History

The card you used last has an accent border, and the OBD button takes you straight back to that page next time. The connection status and log are not on this page: they are in Settings > OBD > Connection.

How it is built

frontend/OBDHome.qml. A tap on a card saves the page's file name in the lastOBDPage setting and opens it. The bottom bar reads the same setting, and ignores it while no car is connected, so the connect prompt is always what you see then.

Live Data

Live Data shows one card for every reading your car supports, each with its name, value and unit and a bar that shows where the value sits in its range. OCTAVE asks the car what it supports after connecting, so the cards differ from car to car. A long list scrolls. A reading that has not answered yet shows "--".

Tap the RPM card to set up the shift light: colours and flashes at engine speeds you choose (OBD Manager: Shift Light). No other card does anything on a tap.

What the page says when there are no cards:

MessageMeaning
No vehicle connectedNo adapter is connected. Tap Connect Vehicle.
Reading your vehicle's PIDs…The adapter is connected and OCTAVE is waiting for the car to say what it supports.
No supported PIDs reportedThe car did not answer. Check that the ignition is on.

How it is built

frontend/OBDMenu.qml. The list of cards is OBDParameterModel.vehicleParameters, and each card reads its own value with OBDParameterModel.liveValue(id), so a card redraws only when its own reading changes (OBDParameterModel, the vehicle gate). The grid has 2 columns for up to 4 cards, 3 for up to 9, 4 for up to 16 or on a narrow page, and 5 beyond that.

While the page is on screen it asks the car for every card's reading (obdManager.setParameterDemand("cards", ids)) and withdraws the request when you leave. Hidden, the cards stop listening, so the page costs nothing off screen.

The page shows no dashboards of its own. The one exception: a dashboard being edited in the browser editor (Gauges & Dashboards) previews here in place of the cards while it is being edited.

The obdCardStyleCircular setting draws the cards as round dials instead of bars. Nothing in Settings changes it at the moment. The shift light editor is frontend/obd/ShiftLightEditor.qml; its rules live in shiftLightManager.

Trouble Codes

This page reads and clears the codes behind the check-engine light. Opening it reads the car's status once and prints it: how many codes are stored and whether the check-engine light is on.

  • Read DTCs reads the stored trouble codes and prints each with its description.
  • Clear DTCs (red) asks first. Clearing also turns the check-engine light off and erases the freeze frame data (the readings the car saved when the fault happened), and it may affect emissions testing readiness.

Everything is printed as text in the DIAGNOSTIC OUTPUT panel, which keeps the last 50 lines. If no car is connected the panel says so; connect one and come back.

The live readings pause while this page is open and resume when you leave it. It does not read permanent codes.

How it is built

frontend/OBDDiagnostics.qml. Reading codes needs the adapter to itself, so the page calls obdManager.enterDiagnosticMode() when it becomes visible and exitDiagnosticMode() when it is hidden. It does this on visibility, not on creation: the page is built ahead of time and kept alive, and entering the mode while hidden would stop the gauges behind the user's back. The buttons call readDtc() and clearDtc(), and the answers arrive as signals. See OBD Manager: Trouble Codes.

Trip History

OCTAVE records every drive (the black box). This page is where you look at them. It opens from the OBD page's Trip History card and from Settings > OBD > Black Box. A switch under the title chooses between Drives and Trends.

Drives lists every drive, newest first, with its start, duration, distance and top speed. The drive being recorded is on top with a blinking red dot, and REC shows beside the title. A drive that was cut off by a loss of power is marked RECOVERED. The line under the list gives the number of drives and the space they use against the storage cap.

Tap a drive to see it:

  • Eight figures: Duration, Distance, Max speed, Max RPM, Peak coolant, Voltage, Fuel used, Samples.
  • A chip for every reading recorded in that drive. Tap one to chart it.
  • The chart, with the lowest, average and highest value. Pinch to zoom, drag to move while zoomed, use the mouse wheel on a computer, and double-tap to see the whole drive again. Zooming in fetches finer detail for the part you are looking at.

The figures and the chart are in the units the car reported (km/h, °C), not your display units. A drive ends by itself. Deleting and exporting are not on this page; Settings > OBD > Black Box can clear the history (Clear history).

Trends

Trends shows the car's health across drives rather than one drive (what the health numbers are). On the left is a card per health number, with a dot (green for fine, amber for worth a look, grey for no data), the recent average, and a small chart with one point per drive. Tap a card to see it large on the right, over 7 d, 30 d, 90 d or All (30 days to start with). The large chart shades the usual range and marks drives outside it. You can also chart any recorded reading across drives from the chips above the chart. Tap a point to open that drive.

How it is built

frontend/TripHistory.qml. Everything is asked for and answered later, so the page never waits on the database: it calls tripRecorder.requestDetail(), requestSeries(), requestHealthTrends() and requestParamTrend() and fills in when the matching signal arrives. Answers for a window or reading you have since left are ignored. Full list: tripRecorder. Test: tst_triphistoryview.

Sensors

The Sensors page shows the motion sensor's readings as cards: PITCH, ROLL, HEADING (in degrees with the compass point), G-force (sideways, forward and total), ALTITUDE and TEMPERATURE. The temperature card is left out for a sensor that has no thermometer.

While the sensor is connected, two buttons sit under the cards. ZERO makes the way the vehicle sits now count as level; use it parked on flat ground. RESET undoes that.

3D View

The 3D View shows your vehicle as a 3D model that tilts as the real one does, with pitch and roll as two large numbers under it and the heading above it. It is an inclinometer you can read at a glance.

The first time, the page asks you to Choose your vehicle: a tile per bundled vehicle (a Jeep TJ and a BMW 330i ZHP) and Add your own 3D model (.glb). After that:

  • Drag to look around the vehicle, pinch or use the mouse wheel to zoom.
  • On a bundled vehicle, tap a door, the hood or the tailgate to open or close it. A model of your own is shown whole and nothing on it opens.
  • With a car connected, a bundled vehicle is driven by the real speed and RPM: its wheels turn as the car's do.
  • The three dots at the top right open a menu: Change vehicle, and, while the motion sensor is connected, Set level here and Reset level (the same as ZERO and RESET on the Sensors page).

Without a motion sensor the page reads "No motion sensor" and shows the vehicle level.

How it is built

FileJob
frontend/CarMenu.qmlThe page: the readouts, the menu, and the wiring from the motion sensor and the car to the model.
frontend/VehicleView.qmlThe 3D scene and its gestures, a component any page can place. See Components: VehicleView.

The model is turned by the sensor's orientation as a quaternion (a four-number rotation that has no awkward angles); pitch, roll and heading in degrees are used for the numbers only. The page listens to the sensor and asks the car for RPM and SPEED only while it is visible, because it stays alive when hidden. The page needs the Qt Quick 3D module and does not load without it.

Clock

Tap the clock on the bottom bar for a full-screen clock: an analog face with a second hand, the time in large digits, and the date. The digits follow Settings > Display > Clock (Time Format and Show Seconds).

How it is built

frontend/ClockMenu.qml, drawn in the theme's colours. It keeps its own one-second timer, which runs only while the page is visible and re-aims itself at each second boundary so the second hand does not lag or skip. It does not use the clock manager: that one announces a change only when the bottom bar's text changes, which without seconds is once a minute.

Phone Mirror

Nothing in the app opens this page for now. An Android phone is shown on Home and in the Media Room instead, and a new phone is paired in Settings > Phone. The page is still built and can be opened by the developer tools.

iPhone (AirPlay)

This page opens by itself when an iPhone starts mirroring to OCTAVE over AirPlay, and shows the iPhone's screen. Before that it is the setup page: Turn on AirPlay, the steps to mirror the screen, the PIN to type on the iPhone the first time, and Pair touch, which lets your taps on OCTAVE's screen reach the iPhone. While mirroring, Calibrate touch fixes taps that land in the wrong place, and X leaves the page without stopping the iPhone.

How it is built

frontend/AirPlayView.qml, over airPlayManager. Main.qml opens it when the manager reports that mirroring started. Setup and how touch works: iPhone (AirPlay).

Android Auto

The Android Auto page shows Android Auto from your phone and passes touches to it. Its button is on the bottom bar only when Android Auto is turned on in Settings > Phone. While it is not running the page says what it is waiting for ("Waiting for the phone", "Starting Android Auto...", "Android Auto stopped") with the one-time setup to do on the phone, and it retries by itself. X stops Android Auto and leaves the page.

How it is built

frontend/AndroidAutoView.qml has two modes, chosen by androidAutoManager.backend. With "native", OCTAVE's own receiver draws the video in the page; the phone stops sending video while the page is hidden, and audio carries on. With "dhu", Google's Desktop Head Unit runs as a separate program and its window is placed inside the page; if it cannot start, the page shows "Could not start Android Auto" with setup instructions and Try Again. Both are explained on Phone Mirror & Android Auto; the full list of calls is androidAutoManager.

How Pages Are Built

From here on the page is for people changing the code.

Every page is a QML file at the top of frontend/, shown in one StackView in Main.qml. The important idea is the page cache: a page is built once, on its first visit or ahead of time in the background, and the same instance is shown again on every later visit. That makes a tap on the bottom bar cheap, and it has three consequences for anyone writing a page:

  • Open pages with stackView.openPage("Foo.qml"). It builds or reuses the instance and passes it stackView and mainWindow. It returns the page, or null if the file would not load.
  • Do per-visit work in StackView.onActivated, not in Component.onCompleted, which runs once, possibly while the page is still hidden.
  • A hidden page is still alive. Stop timers, animations, polling requests and signal handlers that cost something while the page is not visible. Every page above does this.

More on the cache, pre-building and routing: Frontend Overview: the page cache and Page Routing.

The names matter. The bottom bar works out which section you are in from the current page's objectName: a name that starts with obd is the OBD section, one that starts with sensor, or carMenu, is the Sensors section. A new page in one of those sections must follow the pattern, or the button's highlight and its "tapped again" behaviour will not apply to it. See objectName Conventions.

The developer tools and the command server open any of these fifteen pages by file name with mainWindow.navigateTo("Foo.qml"). A new page needs a row in that function's table in Main.qml.

Pages size things with a local dp() function rather than fixed pixels, so they scale with the UI Scale setting: see GUI Scale. Colours come from Style (Theme System).

Tests: tst_dashboardwidgets loads every Home screen, tst_triphistoryview drives Trip History against a fake recorder, and tst_conformance fails if any QML uses a manager or member the published API does not list.

Why It Is Built This Way

  • A hub page in front of the OBD and sensor pages. The bottom bar needs somewhere stable to land, and a place for the connect prompt, while still jumping straight to the page you used last.
  • The last page is remembered only after it has opened. If the hub saved the name first and the page then failed to load (the 3D View without Qt Quick 3D, for one), the Sensors button would do nothing on every later start. For the same reason the bottom bar clears a remembered page that will not load and falls back to the hub.
  • Trouble Codes pauses the live readings by visibility. A page under another page, or pre-built, is neither destroyed nor visible. Tying diagnostic mode to visibility means the gauges resume the moment you leave.
  • Home's prompts sit over the widgets. A fresh install is then one tap from a car and one from music, instead of a screen of "--".
  • The look editor is inside the page it edits. You see every change on the real Media Room, there is no second copy to keep in step, and the way back is a tap on the page itself.
  • The phone pages recover by themselves. A nudged cable is the normal failure in a car. The last picture stays up and the session restarts when the phone is back; setup instructions are kept for when something really is missing.
