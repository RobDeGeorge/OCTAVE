What It Is

The bottom bar is the strip of buttons that stays on screen whatever page you are on. It is how you move between the sections of OCTAVE: Home, OBD, Media, Sensors and Settings. It also shows the clock, and it can carry the music keys and the volume.

The bar sits along the bottom of the screen, or down the left side if you prefer. The pages fill the rest. It fits itself to any window: on a small one it drops the least important controls first and always keeps the section buttons.

This page starts with how to use it and gets more technical as it goes.

 Along the bottom:
 +--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
 | [<<] [>||] [>>] [Shuf] [Mute] 50%    [Home] [OBD] [Media] [Sens] [Set]    12:34 |
 +--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
   music keys and volume (optional)      section buttons, centred           clock

 Down the left side: the same groups from top to bottom, with the music keys
 in rows (previous / play / next, then shuffle / mute, then the volume).

The Section Buttons

There are five section buttons, plus Android Auto when you turn it on. The button of the section you are in has a faint tinted fill. A tap changes the page at once, with no animation.

ButtonWhat a tap does
HomeGoes back to Home. If you are already on Home and a phone is being mirrored, it presses the phone's own Home key.
OBDFrom another section: reopens the OBD page you last used, or OBD Home if there is none. With no car connected it always opens OBD Home, which then shows the connect prompt. On an OBD page: goes back to OBD Home. On OBD Home: nothing.
MediaFrom another section: opens the Media Room, or the library if "Open Music to the Library" is on. On either of the two: switches to the other.
SensorsFrom another section: reopens the sensor page you last used, or Sensor Home. On a sensor page or the 3D vehicle view: goes back to Sensor Home. On Sensor Home: nothing. If the remembered page can no longer be opened (the 3D view needs a module that may be missing), OCTAVE forgets it and opens Sensor Home.
SettingsOpens Settings where you left it. A tap while in Settings goes back to the settings grid and closes the open card. Press and hold to choose which settings sections are shown.
Android AutoOpens the Android Auto screen. Shown only when Settings > Phone > Android Auto > Show in Nav Bar is on.

There is no Phone Mirror button. A mirrored phone is shown on Home and in the Media Room; see Phone Mirror & Android Auto.

An orange dot on the Settings button means an update is available. It pulses five times, then stays lit. Settings > About has the update itself; see Network Manager.

Press and hold Settings: Toggle View

Holding the Settings button opens a panel called Toggle View, with a switch for each settings section: Device, Media, Display, OBD, Phone, Accessories and About (on Android, where Settings has no Phone or Accessories section, those two are left out). Turn one off and that section is hidden from Settings. Tap Close or outside the panel when done. The choice is saved; how Settings uses it is on the Settings UI page.

Music Keys

The music keys are off on a fresh install. Turn them on in Settings > Display > Layout > Nav Bar Media Controls. You then get Previous, Play / Pause, Next, Shuffle, Mute and the volume in percent.

The keys control whatever is playing: OCTAVE's own player, or the phone, Android Auto or Spotify while one of those has the music. Shuffle is lit while it is on. It works for OCTAVE's own player and for Spotify; for any other app on the phone it does nothing, because that shuffle belongs to the app. The Shuffle key is drawn dim only then, when it does nothing.

Volume

The volume is part of the music keys group, so it shows only when that group is on.

  • The percentage. Tap it and a wide slider opens across the screen. Drag anywhere along it; you do not have to hit the handle. Tap outside to close it.
  • Mute. Tap to mute, tap again to unmute. The icon shows a crossed-out speaker while muted or at 0 %, and otherwise a speaker with one, two or three waves: below 33 %, below 66 %, and from 66 % up.
  • Turning the volume up while muted unmutes, from the slider, a knob or a gesture alike.
  • Tapping Mute at 0 % brings the volume back to the startup volume, since there is nothing to unmute to.
  • Mute is not saved: OCTAVE always starts unmuted.

The bar is one of several places that set the volume; the volume knob, the gesture sensor and the Media Room are others. They all go through one object, volumeController, so the number on the bar follows whichever one you used. The bar reads volumeController.percent and effectivelyMuted and calls applyVolume(percent) and toggleMute(). It does no volume math of its own. See volumeController in the API reference.

Clock

The clock is at the right end of the bar, or at the bottom of a side bar. Tap it to open the Clock page. Its format, seconds and size are set in Settings > Display > Clock (below). With Clock Size on Auto, the digits stand as tall as the icons in the buttons. With Auto off, the size you set is an upper limit: the clock still never grows past the bar and shrinks when room is short.

Swiping Between Sections

You can also move between sections by swiping sideways on a page. Swipe with your finger moving left and you get the section whose button is to the right of the current one; swipe right for the one to the left. It does what a tap on that button would do, and the new page slides in over the old one.

What counts as a swipe:

  • It is quick: under 0.7 seconds from touch to lift.
  • It is long: at least 18 % of the page's width (and never less than a fixed minimum on a small window).
  • It is clearly sideways: at least twice as far sideways as up or down.

What a swipe does not do:

  • It does not wrap. Swiping past Home or past the last button does nothing.
  • It does nothing on a page that belongs to no button, such as the Clock page.
  • It leaves alone anything that drags sideways itself. A swipe that starts on a slider, a list that scrolls sideways, the seek bar, the mirrored phone's screen, the 3D vehicle (which you turn by dragging), a zoomed Trip History chart or the Android Auto screen belongs to that control and never changes the page. Start the swipe somewhere else on the page.

There is no setting to turn swiping off.

How It Is Built

From here on the page is for people changing the code.

A bar like this has one hard problem: it must look right and stay usable at every window size, in two orientations, with groups of controls that can be switched off. OCTAVE solves it by separating two jobs. frontend/BottomBar.qml holds one copy of each control and what it does. frontend/BottomBarLayout.js is a single function, compute(), that takes the bar's size and what is switched on and returns a rectangle for every control. The QML only puts each control at its rectangle. Because the layout is a plain function with no screen behind it, a test can run it over hundreds of sizes.

The bar is created once, in frontend/Main.qml, next to the StackView that holds the pages. It talks to the rest of the app through a few context properties: nowPlaying for the music keys, volumeController for volume and mute, clock for the time, settingsManager for its settings, networkManager for the update dot and phoneMirrorManager for the phone's Home key.

The Bar's Size and Place

The bar's thickness is a share of the window, held between two limits so it is never too thin to touch and never a thick slab on a tall or very wide window.

PositionThicknessBut at leastOn a 1280 × 800 screen
Bottom12.5 % of the window's height, at most 9 % of its width56 scaled pixels (never under 44), at most 20 % of the height100 px
Side10 % of the window's width, at most 16 % of its height72 scaled pixels (never under 56), at most 20 % of the width128 px

The bar's position and size are bindings on the window's size and the orientation setting. Nothing assigns them from a handler, so a resize or a switch of position cannot leave a stale size behind. The pages' StackView is anchored to the bar's inner edge and takes the rest of the window.

The shadow strips. Main.qml draws a 12 px shadow along the bar's inner edge: above a bottom bar, to the right of a side bar. The strip lies over the edge of the page area and is drawn above the pages, so the last 12 px of every page on that side are under it. The order from back to front is the bar, the pages, the shadow.

Switching position while Settings is open. The Settings page was laid out for the old page area, so Main.qml builds a fresh one a moment later, on the same section and the same open card.

Spacing.bottomBarHeight is not the bar's real thickness. It is a spacing token the settings pages use for padding at their bottom. See Theme System for the tokens.

Fitting the Controls

Everything on the bar is sized as a multiple of one unit: the side of a section button. So the bar grows and shrinks as a whole and keeps its proportions.

ControlSize in units
Section button1 × 1. On a side bar it is up to 1.4 wide, never wider than the bar.
Music key1 × 1, the same as a section button. On a side bar: square, three to a row.
Icon in a button0.7 of the button
Volume text0.36
Clock on AutoDigits 0.7 tall, the same as the icons; may shrink to 0.45
Gaps0.3 at the ends, 0.6 between groups, 0.3 between buttons, each with a smaller minimum

The unit starts at 75 % of a bottom bar's thickness, times the UI scale, and is never more than 85 % of the thickness. On a side bar it comes from the bar's width. Then the controls are fitted along the bar's length in this order:

  1. Full size, comfortable gaps. The music keys go at the start, the clock at the end, and the section buttons at the centre of the bar. If the other groups leave no room at the centre, the section buttons sit between them.
  2. Tighten the gaps. First the gaps at the ends and between groups, then the gaps between buttons. Then the clock gives up some size.
  3. Shrink everything together.
  4. Drop controls. If shrinking would take the section buttons below the smallest allowed size, controls are removed in this order and the fit starts again: shuffle, the volume text, mute, previous / play / next, the clock. Section buttons are never dropped.

The smallest allowed button is 4.5 % of the window's shorter side, and at least 24 px. A control that would be too small on its own is dropped by itself: the clock when its text would be under about 12 px, and previous / play / next when they cannot fit across a narrow side bar.

Touch areas. Each control's touch area is larger than what is drawn: half a unit more along the bar, and the bar's full thickness across it. Where two would overlap, the line between them is put halfway between the two controls. Every press lands on exactly one control, and the areas are as large as that allows.

The clock does not jiggle. The layout measures the clock's text with every digit replaced by 8, so the bar does not shift as the minutes change.

The layout promises, and the test checks at every size, that:

  • every control and every touch area lies inside the bar;
  • no two controls overlap and no two touch areas overlap;
  • each control lies inside its own touch area.

Which Section Is Current

Every page has an objectName. The bar looks at the page on top of the stack and maps its name to a section. That one value, bottomBar.activeSection, lights the button and tells the swipe where it is.

Page's objectNameSection
mainMenuhome
anything starting with obdobd
mediaRoom, mediaPlayermedia
anything starting with sensor, and carMenusensor
settingsMenusettings
androidAutoViewandroidAuto
anything elsenone: no button is lit and a swipe does nothing

The buttons on the bar, in order, are bottomBar.navSections. A swipe moves one step along that list from activeSection and calls the same function a tap on that button calls.

Taps and the Swipe Slide

  • Section buttons act on touch-down, not on release, so the page changes without waiting for the finger to lift. The clock does the same.
  • Settings acts on release, because it also has press-and-hold. A hold opens Toggle View and does not open Settings.
  • The page change waits about two frames. The button's squish must be on screen before the page work starts, or a fast tap would show no feedback at all. If no frame comes, it goes ahead after 80 ms.
  • The squish runs on the render thread, so it keeps moving while the main thread is busy opening a page. The price: it plays in full on every tap and does not hold while the finger stays down. The music keys do no page work, so they simply squish while pressed and act on release.
  • A tap is an instant cut; a swipe slides. The page stack has no transitions except during a swipe. Then the new page comes in from the side over 320 ms, on top of the old one, which drifts back, shrinks a little and dims over black.
  • Pages opened from the bar are built once and reused; see the page cache.

Adding a Button

All of it is in frontend/BottomBar.qml:

  1. Put a single-colour SVG icon in frontend/assets/. The bar tints it.
  2. Write the function a tap runs, such as openMySection(). Open the page with stackView.openPage("MyPage.qml") so it is cached.
  3. Add a row to navButtons: mySection: { icon: "./assets/my_button.svg", open: openMySection }.
  4. Add the name to navSections, at the place it should have on the bar. That is also its place in the swipe order. Add it under a condition if a setting should show or hide it.
  5. Add a case to navColor() for the icon's colour. Without one the icon is drawn in the theme's accent colour.
  6. Add a rule to activeSection that maps your page's objectName to the section. Without it the button never lights and you cannot swipe away from the page.

The layout needs no change: it takes the number of buttons and makes room. Run ctest -R tst_bottombarlayout if you touch the proportions. The Phone Mirror button's row, colour and function are still in the file, unused; adding "phoneMirror" to navSections would bring it back.

Keeping a Swipe for Your Own Item

When a touch starts, Main.qml (pageSwipe) walks from the page down to the item under the finger. If any item on the way drags sideways itself, the touch is not a page swipe. These are recognised without any work from you:

  • a horizontal Slider or RangeSlider;
  • a Flickable, ListView or PathView that is interactive and scrolls sideways (a list that only scrolls up and down does not count);
  • a MultiPointTouchArea;
  • a MouseArea with preventStealing: true.

Anything else needs one line on the item:

readonly property bool octaveNoPageSwipe: true

You need it for an item that drags with a pointer handler such as DragHandler, which cannot be seen from outside, and for an area that should never change the page even when its own control is switched off. The property counts on a disabled item too; the automatic cases above count only while the item is enabled. It can be a binding: the Trip History chart sets it only while zoomed. The phone mirror widget, the full-screen phone page, the 3D vehicle and the Android Auto screen all set it.

The swipe watcher only observes. It never takes a touch away from the page, so adding it changed nothing about how pages receive presses. Each swipe is logged, for example [NAV] Swipe left to obd.

tst_bottombarlayout runs compute() with no screen, over a grid of bar sizes (bottom: 320 to 3840 wide and 32 to 220 thick; side: 40 to 320 wide and 240 to 2160 long), with 5 and 7 section buttons and every combination of music keys, clock and Auto clock. At each size it checks the three promises above. It also checks UI scales from 0.5 to 2, a 500 px manual clock, a bar of zero size, that a 1280 × 800 screen keeps every control, that shuffle is the first control dropped, that the section buttons are one size and evenly spaced, and that along the bottom the music keys match the section buttons. The swipe has no automated test.

Why It Is Built This Way

  • One set of controls, placed by one function. Two hand-written layouts with fixed percentages will run controls into each other at some window size. With one function of the bar's size, "nothing overlaps and nothing leaves the bar" is something a test can prove over hundreds of sizes.
  • Everything is a multiple of one unit. The bar scales as a whole, so it looks the same on a phone and on a wide desktop window.
  • Drop controls before shrinking the section buttons too far. Navigation is the bar's job. A shuffle key is not worth a section button too small to hit in a moving car.
  • The smallest button follows the window, not the screen. The app's scaled pixels follow the screen, so a small window on a large monitor would drop controls it has room for.
  • Size and place by bindings. Assigning sizes from handlers breaks bindings, and a switch of position could then leave the bar with no width.
  • Touch areas as large as they can be without overlapping. A car touchscreen is used at arm's length on a rough road.
  • Buttons act on touch-down. Waiting for release adds about a tenth of a second to every page change.
  • A tap on Settings never opens a panel by surprise. Toggle View is on press-and-hold; a tap inside Settings goes back to the grid.
  • The remembered sensor page is checked. A remembered page that cannot load would make the button look dead, so it is forgotten and Sensor Home opens.
  • The swipe only watches. It never takes a touch from a page, and it gives way to anything that drags sideways, so no control was changed to make room for it.
  • The update dot stops pulsing. An animation that runs for as long as an update is available keeps the screen repainting for days.
  • The bar's file keeps its own two-line scaling helpers. Calling functions on a shared QML singleton has been unreliable on Android; reading its properties is fine.
