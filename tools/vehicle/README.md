# Photo-referenced 2003 Jeep TJ Sahara

## Owner's target configuration

The owner's digital-twin target is a **2003 TJ with front and rear Dana 44s,
a 3.5-inch lift, 31-inch BFGoodrich KO2 tires, Fox shocks, black 16-inch Method
wheels, a stock 4.0 L inline six, five-speed manual and a soft top**.
The machine-readable source is `target_vehicle.json`; each new candidate
preserves a copy and reports known configuration gaps.

This specification supersedes the older visual target described below. It is
not yet implemented in the geometry: the current generator still makes a
hardtop, generic wheels/shocks and the prior static chassis. Soft-top style,
Method/Fox models, lift construction, axle ratio and measured hardpoints remain
unconfirmed. A nominal 3.5-inch lift is 0.0889 m; it must not be applied as a
uniform scale or a whole-vehicle translation. Suspension and body-lift changes
affect different mounts and assemblies. The soft top also needs its own window
and folding behavior instead of retaining the hardtop's hinged glass animation.

## Existing generated asset

There is now a separate **lifted soft-top Blender pose prototype**, generated by
`build_jeep_twin.py`. It replaces the hardtop with fabric/vinyl panels, bow tubes,
seams and window retainers, raises sprung assemblies by 0.0889 m, separates both
axles, and adds heave/roll, wheel/crank rotation and opening-panel controls.
Shocks telescope with fixed body/shaft lengths; springs compress between mounts.
Select `JEEP_CONTROLS` and edit Custom Properties; frames 1–120 demonstrate poses.
The `.blend` contains a `START HERE - Jeep rig` text block.

The soft top now uses a shared, welded fabric/vinyl envelope, including the rear
roof closure and door-top valances. Seams follow the roof surface and bows sit
below it. The control check also verifies connected topology, closed thickness,
absence of roof-join boundaries and nonadjacent surface crossings.
`audit_jeep_fit.py` checks roof/bows, tires/flares, shocks/springs, shocks/tires,
shocks/frame and hood/engine surface intersections at frames 1, 40, 80 and 120.
The candidate loop fails when these selected intersection checks fail. This is
not an exhaustive collision certificate, nor does it validate mount dimensions.
Shock mounts remain provisional; the fit pass relocates them to avoid the coil
and tire intersections found in the earlier prototype.

`cabin.py` replaces the old front upholstery blocks with contoured cushions and
rearward-reclining, integrated seatbacks. Insert materials share the seat mesh.
It adds manual/transfer-case boots and levers, a five-speed knob, a larger wheel
aligned with its 20-degree column, a cut column aperture, and distinct dash
controls. Dimensions are photo estimates. Cached reference photos 048, 053 and
067 guide the seats/wheel; manual-specific component layout is checked against
the [2003 TJ gearshift parts diagram](https://www.moparpartsgiant.com/parts-list/2003-jeep-wrangler/manual_transmission/gear_shift_controls.html)
and a [2003 five-speed vehicle reference](https://carsandbids.com/auctions/3oe5DB8o/2003-jeep-wrangler-sport-4x4).
These are shape/layout references, not proof of the owner's exact trim.

Cabin checks cover seat direction, wheel/column alignment, manual-control
inventory, and selected seat/console/body, steering/dash, pedal, and shifter
intersections. With `--twin --preview`, the loop also renders exposed cabin
views and saves `cabin-inspection.blend`, a separate copy with the roof, doors
and roll bars hidden. The complete `jeep-twin.blend` retains the full vehicle.

This is not the app export yet. It assumes a suspension lift and framed black
squareback top pending confirmation. Links currently stretch under prescribed
axle poses; the nine-pose sweep reports the maximum length error. Steering is a
pose control, not the existing Qt linkage solver; driveshaft articulation and
powertrain coupling remain unfinished. Wheels remain generic, shock dimensions
are provisional, and the soft top does not yet fold. Do not certify the prototype
as mechanically accurate or replace the app's GLBs with it.

Soft-top component layout is informed by the
[Bestop TJ Supertop installation diagrams](https://www.bestop.com/wp-content/uploads/Installation%20Instructions/BDKW_51709-INS_001.pdf).
This establishes hardware relationships, not the owner's exact top model or
measured surface dimensions. Geometry and fabric shading are generated locally.

`frontend/assets/jeep_tj_2003.glb` is an original model reconstructed manually
from photographs of one 2003 TJ Sahara: Shale Green paint, Dark Khaki hardtop,
full doors with lowered windows, Canyon wheels, 31-inch tires, and Khaki cabin.
The app model uses silver metallic paint, a black hardtop, black upholstery,
textured black Sport-style flares, left-hand-drive controls, and 31-inch
KO2-inspired tires on 16-inch satin-black eight-spoke rims, as requested.
The spare matches the road wheels. The reference vehicle remains Shale Green.

The [reference guide](../../docs/vehicle/references.md) records the selected
angles, the corrections they informed, and a manifest of all 164 downloaded
reference photos from the same vehicle. This is a photo-based reconstruction;
**dimensional 1:1 accuracy is not established**. The 2.372 m wheelbase is anchored
to published dimensions; surfaces and small details are interpreted from images.

All geometry and the studio light probe are generated locally from source under
the repository license. No third-party meshes or photographic textures are
bundled. Reference photography remains in the ignored `dev/jeep-tj-reference/`
working cache, separate from the app's assets.

## Rebuild / edit

### Digital-twin development loop

Run a reproducible candidate without overwriting the app's assets:

```sh
venv/bin/python tools/vehicle/iterate_jeep.py
# Also render front-quarter, side, front, and rear views:
venv/bin/python tools/vehicle/iterate_jeep.py --preview
```

Each run creates `dev/jeep-twin/candidates/<UTC timestamp>/` with an editable
`jeep-tj.blend`, the complete exported asset set, stage logs, source/artifact
SHA-256 hashes and `report.json`. The harness runs Blender, steering constraints,
export geometry checks and panel-return clearance checks. A failed stage produces
a nonzero exit status. Blender Python exceptions also fail the stage. Supply
`--output` for a new candidate directory, `--python` for a Python with NumPy,
or `--blender` to select a Blender executable. Existing directories are rejected
to preserve prior evidence.

This is an execution harness for an agent-driven loop, not an unattended agent
or a geometry optimizer. The agent's workflow is:

1. Select one mechanical or visual defect and define its measurable acceptance
   check. Record the reference, units, measurement uncertainty and configuration.
2. Edit the generator/rig/runtime source; build a new isolated candidate.
3. Read every failed-stage log, fix the cause and rerun. Do not weaken a check
   just to make a candidate pass.
4. Inspect renders against matching reference camera angles. Validate the actual
   moving assembly throughout its travel, including combined steering/travel.
5. Review the candidate in Qt and measure target-device performance before
   promoting its complete asset set. Repeat for the next unmet requirement.

`baseline_checks_passed` means only that the existing regression checks pass.
It does **not** certify a digital twin. Panel checking currently covers selected
body returns, not all possible collisions. Qt's current test uses the installed
app assets, so the candidate report explicitly leaves Qt integration unverified.
The harness does not auto-promote assets or change the user's app/UI work.

The next acceptance gates, in dependency order, are:

| Gate | Required evidence before calling it complete |
| --- | --- |
| Vehicle specification | Confirm actual trim, axle assemblies/ratios, transfer case, suspension modifications, tire dimensions and reference measurements. Existing values remain assumptions until verified. |
| Suspension | Separate axle assemblies; measured chassis/axle hardpoints; linked control arms and track bars; springs and shocks that follow travel; steering and driveshaft articulation; bounded joint/length residuals and clearance over a travel grid. |
| Steering and tires | Cabin wheel/column follows linkage; wheel trajectories and rolling speeds agree during turns; steering works across suspension travel; brakes stay attached to their correct assemblies. |
| Powertrain | Explicit clutch, neutral, five forward gears, reverse and transfer-case states; documented ratios; consistent crank/transmission/shaft/wheel speeds; belt and accessory coupling. Distinguish true RPM from slowed visualization. |
| Engine internals | If requested: crank, six pistons/rods and valve train driven by one crank phase, verified stroke/phase/clearance, and an inspection cutaway. |
| Remaining mechanisms | Inventory and validate windows, latches, wipers, pedals, shifters, parking brake, seats, lights and removable panels; define what is animated versus simulated. |
| Appearance | Matched-angle reference comparisons, measured silhouette and panel gaps, underside/engine/cabin inspection, materials under multiple lighting conditions. |
| Delivery | Blender controls and named hierarchy, deterministic exports, Qt motion parity, device-specific frame/memory budgets and a configuration/accuracy report. |

The Blender source rig and Qt runtime must share hardpoints and mechanical state.
Blender constraints/drivers should be evaluated or baked for exports; the app's
interactive mechanisms need equivalent runtime solvers, since a GLB alone does
not carry Blender's constraint evaluation into Qt.

Use Blender 4.2 or later; 5.2 also builds it (the app itself does not require Blender):

```sh
blender --background --python tools/vehicle/build_jeep_tj.py
blender --background --python tools/vehicle/build_jeep_tj.py -- \
  --blend /tmp/jeep-tj.blend --preview /tmp/jeep-tj.png
python3 tools/vehicle/build_studio_probe.py
```

Alternatively, run the model builder with Python 3.11 and `bpy==4.2.0` installed
in an isolated environment. `--output` overrides the GLB path. The optional
`.blend` preserves individually named editable parts before material batching.
`--preview` renders front-quarter, side, front, and rear-quarter PNGs.

Edit the `paint` and `roof` materials near the top of the builder for color
changes. The model includes its cabin, so the lowered door windows expose real
interior geometry. Windshield and rear glass use glTF alpha blending. This is
supported in the tested desktop Qt runtime; inspect transparency on target GPUs.

The full GLB includes five opening hinges/clips, two steering-knuckle pivots, four axle-spin pivots,
four engine/driveline spinners, and a neutral steering-link assembly.
Geometry is batched by material within each part. `assets/jeep_tj/` contains separate
part-local GLBs, plus generated `Rig.js` and `rig.json` pivot/bounds metadata.
`JeepVehicle.qml` assembles these parts using public Qt APIs, avoiding imported
scene internals. Rebuild and distribute the complete generated asset set together.

The view provides controls for both front doors, hood, rear glass, tailgate and
headlights. “Open all” opens all five hinged panels for inspection, preserving
the rear-glass/tailgate sequence; “Close all” shuts them again. Mirrors and interior cards follow their doors; the spare and third
brake light follow the tailgate. The tailgate opens before the rear glass and
closes after it. Headlights combine visible emissive lenses with spotlights.
Door jambs follow only the sides and sill, leaving the entry unobstructed. Front
seats have floor feet, bolted brackets and slide rails; the rear bench has fixed
support legs and rails. The pedals hang beneath the driver dashboard, forward of
the seat. The left rear fuel filler sits clear of the flare, including its bezel. The
hood has a shallow deck crown and narrow shoulder roll; the cowl sides are flat
and flush with the door jambs, with a separate firewall behind the hinged hood.
The tub floor uses thin footwell pans, a raised transmission tunnel, rear wheel
housings, a stepped cargo floor, reinforcing ribs and body mounts. Two recessed
cupholders sit between the shifter and armrest alongside the parking brake.

The fenders have tapered ends and closed, rolled cross-sections. Exterior skins
have thickness at the rear quarters, windshield and hardtop. Curved corner
returns join the rear tub and hardtop panels; recessed door flanges and seals
back the shut lines without closing the door apertures. Windshield side returns
connect the raked frame to the front door pillars. The rear roof sections meet
the hardtop corner and rear-window surround. Front and rear
Dana 44 carriers have angular ten-bolt covers, fill plugs and pinion housings;
the front carrier is offset toward the driver and the rear is centered.

The steering slider drives the pitman arm and solves two fixed-length constraints
for the drag link and bent tie rod in an inverted-Y layout. Each wheel, brake and
steering arm rotates together about its knuckle; the inside wheel turns farther.
The Spin wheels button and signed speed slider drive all four road wheels in
forward or reverse, using the 31-inch tire circumference. Stopping holds the
current rotation. Front tire/rim/hub/rotor parts spin under their steering
pivots; calipers and steering arms only steer. Rear wheels spin about their
axle centers, and the spare stays fixed to its carrier. All four wheels use
the same test speed; differential speed in turns is not simulated. The full
GLB exports separate pivots; Qt composes the front steering/spin hierarchy.
The damper telescopes between its axle bracket and tie-rod clamp. Hardpoints in
`steering_geometry.json` are approximate visual dimensions. Suspension travel,
load-dependent steering and cabin steering-wheel rotation are not animated.
The full GLB contains the links in their neutral pose; Qt replaces that part with
`JeepLink.qml` geometry driven by `JeepSteering.js` for live steering.

The 3D-view toolbar independently toggles headlights, fog lights, brake lights
and reverse lights. `JeepLamp.qml` adds emissive lens surfaces and directional
light spill for the fog, brake and reverse circuits. The high-mounted brake
lamp is a child of the tailgate hinge; the two lower brake/reverse assemblies
stay attached to the tub. These are manual test controls; hardware brake/gear
inputs are not connected.

The five tires use a 0.3937 m tread radius (31 inches overall), interlocking
siped blocks, shoulder armor and raised black BFGoodrich / KO2 lettering.
The black rims use a 0.2032 m bead-seat radius (16 inches), with larger outer
protective lips, connected barrels, eight spokes and five lug nuts. This is a
photo-interpreted tread at the requested dimensions, not a retail tire SKU.

The shocks are detailed static geometry, including bodies, chrome pistons, boots,
bushings and mounts; suspension travel is not animated.

## Engine and driveline

The drivetrain matches the owner's TJ: a 4.0 L AMC inline six, an NV3550
five-speed manual, and an NP231 transfer case. Sizes come from published
figures (4.40 in bore spacing, 9.13 in deck height). Placement follows the
engine-bay photographs (114-126, 218-258): the air box, battery, alternator and
PCM are on the passenger side; the intake and exhaust manifolds are both on the
driver side (the head is not crossflow), with the throttle body, brake booster,
master cylinder and coolant/washer bottles; and the intake tube arches over the
valve cover. The engine has a block with freeze plugs, head, valve cover, coil
rail, oil pan with rear sump, timing cover, an accessory drive with a convex-hull
serpentine belt, fan clutch, seven-blade fan and shroud, and radiator hoses.
Everything rigid to the block is built level and then tilted 3 degrees tail-down
about the crank nose. The bellhousing passes through a notch in the firewall
into a taller transmission tunnel, and the NV3550 shifter tower sits under the
console. The NP231's front output is offset low on the driver side, feeding the
driver-side front differential. The reference vehicle is an automatic, so the
transmission follows the owner's manual rather than the photos.

Four parts spin: `crank_pulley`, `engine_fan` (fan, clutch and water pump
pulley), `front_driveshaft` and `rear_driveshaft` (tube, slip and pinion yokes,
two U-joints). Their axes are tilted, so the exporter bakes each mesh in a local
frame whose Qt +Z is the shaft axis (all pointing forward). `rig.json` / `Rig.js`
give `axis: "Z"` plus an `orient` quaternion `[w, x, y, z]` (Qt coordinates), and
`JeepHinge` composes `orient × spin`. `JeepVehicle` turns the driveshafts at
wheel speed × `axleRatio` (3.73) and the crank and fan at `engineRpm`, slowed 60×
so a 7-blade fan does not strobe at display frame rates. Both rotate clockwise
seen from the front. CarMenu's Engine slider sets `engineRpm`; with OBD
connected, live RPM and SPEED drive it instead. Belt and accessory pulleys other
than the crank and water pump are static.

Small fasteners, fins and ribs pass `bevel=0` to `cylinder()` / `bar()` /
`box()`. The rounded edges are invisible at that size and roughly double the
triangle count (the drivetrain adds about 35k triangles; the full GLB is 17.9 MB).

The exported GLBs embed their data. The separate
128 KiB `vehicle_studio.hdr` provides environment reflections so the enamel,
glass and metal remain readable as the vehicle rotates. It is generated with
standard-library Python and contains no external imagery.

## Coordinate contract

- GLB units: metres. +Y up, +Z forward, +X vehicle left.
- Origin: midway between the axles, 0.9 m above ground.
- QML scales metres by 30 and applies sensor rotation to the parent node.
- Pitch is around X, yaw around Y, roll around Z, matching the existing sensor
  quaternion mapping. Geometry contains no attitude correction transforms.
- Door hinges rotate around +Y; hood and rear glass rotate around +X.
- No ground plane, camera, or lights are exported. Studio render fixtures are
  created after the GLB export.

## Validation

```sh
node tools/vehicle/check_jeep_steering.cjs
venv/bin/python tools/vehicle/check_jeep_asset.py
QT_QPA_PLATFORM=xcb xvfb-run -a venv/bin/python tools/vehicle/check_jeep_view.py
```

- Panel-fit check: `check_jeep_panel_fit.py` uses the builder’s bpy environment
  and saved `.blend` to check closed return edges and moving-panel clearance.
- Steering checks: 201 input positions, constant rod lengths, monotonic angles,
  inside-wheel ordering and bounded input. Inspected 21 poses for tire/body contact.
- Asset checks: finite vertices, valid indices, matching full/assembled geometry
  (including spinner orientations), five animation clips, attached door
  components, four shocks, the engine/driveline parts and forward spin axes,
  and swept bounds. `check_jeep_asset.py <assets dir>` checks a scratch build.
- Camera fitting uses the generated closed/swept radius, including moving panels.
- Real `CarMenu.qml` tested with Qt 6.11.1 / Xvfb: five animated hinges, rear-opening
  interlock in both directions, all four light circuits, moving third brake lamp,
  sensor quaternion mapping, driveshafts at the axle ratio, crank/fan from the
  Engine slider, fixed spin axes, and live OBD (a fake `obdManager`) taking over
  RPM/speed and the `"carMenu"` poll demand. The view check loads parts through
  the real `backend.asset_store.AssetStore`, as the app does.
- Closed, open, underside, and narrow views captured; studio views regenerated.
- Physical IMU and Android/Raspberry Pi performance have not been tested.

The Tailgate test button controls the complete rear-opening sequence: the gate
finishes opening before the glass rises; on closing, the glass finishes
lowering before the gate shuts. Completion checks include animation state,
and the app test samples both stages and interrupted/reversed requests.

Dashboard revision: `dashboard.py` replaces the original slab/raised-dial dash
with a recessed common instrument panel, six marked gauges, center vent/radio/
HVAC assemblies, outboard vertical vents, passenger airbag, grab handle and
separate glovebox. Proportions reference cached `dev/jeep-tj-reference/048.jpg`;
they are photographic estimates. The parking brake has a cut-out driver-side
console channel and a separate pivot housing. The fit audit now explicitly
checks the brake against console surfaces. This does not yet validate a moving
parking-brake mechanism or provide OEM dimensional accuracy.

Steering reach correction: the cabin wheel/hub/stalk assembly moves 80 mm
forward along its existing 20-degree column axis. The modeled column span
reduces from 295 mm to 215 mm without moving its dash-side endpoint. A tapered
molded housing now surrounds the exposed upper column. These are visual
corrections from the TJ reference, not factory measurements. The cabin render
set includes a dedicated steering-column side view; the clearance audit checks
the shaft and housing against the dashboard and instrument binnacle.

Wheel shape revision: `steering_wheel.py` builds a cyclic rim, broad molded
spoke wings, shaped airbag pad with the lower opening, and inset labeled cruise
switches from reference photos 048/067. It retains the prior column axis and
reach estimate. Cabin renders now support `--views` and `--focus-steering` for
focused visual review. The face-on wheel view hides front seat upholstery for
camera clearance. Wheel geometry tests check closed rim topology and closed
solid pad/spoke/button meshes, in addition to column alignment and selected
clearances. These checks do not certify factory proportions or finish quality.

Wheel proportion pass: reduced rim section from 17 to 14.5 mm radius;
reduced the pad's upper extent from 106 to 62 mm above the wheel center,
widened its shoulders, and lowered the spoke/button assemblies. This restores
the larger upper opening seen in reference 048. Mounting position is unchanged.
These dimensions describe the model revision, not measured TJ factory values.
